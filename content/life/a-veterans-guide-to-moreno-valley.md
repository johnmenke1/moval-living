If you've ever lived in Moreno Valley, you know the sound. It's that low rumble overhead, the sight of a C-17 Globemaster III banking toward the runway at March Air Reserve Base. For some, it's just background noise, but for those of us who've worn the uniform, it's the heartbeat of this town.

As an Army vet, I can tell you that "transitioning" is a word we throw around a lot, but the reality is much more than just a checklist on a DD-214. It's about finding a place where your service is understood without you having to explain it. I've found that here in MoVal. We aren't just a city with a base next door; we're a community where more than 27,000 veterans call home — one of the largest concentrations in the country.

Whether you're fresh out of terminal leave or you've been out for decades, I want to share some of the things I've learned about navigating life here. This isn't a government brochure — it's a guide from one vet to another on how to actually use the resources we earned.

## Getting Back to Work (And Getting Paid for It)

Let's be honest: translating "NCOIC of a motor pool" into a corporate resume can feel like you're trying to speak a foreign language. But Moreno Valley has a program I haven't seen in many other places called **Hire a MoVal Veteran**.

Here's the deal: if a local business hires you and you live here in the city, they get a $1,000 stipend, and **you get a $500 stipend** directly. It's a $1,500 incentive just for being a vet and getting a job. If you're interviewing locally, mention this. It makes you an even more attractive hire because it literally reduces the cost for the employer to bring you onto the team.

If you need help with that resume or want to practice your interview "civilian speak," head over to the **Business & Employment Resource Center (BERC)**. They've got people who specialize in military-to-civilian transitions, and they can connect you with regional employers who are specifically looking for the leadership skills we bring to the table.

## The Classroom Mission: Moreno Valley College

I know the idea of sitting in a classroom after years in the field can be intimidating. Maybe you haven't done a math problem since high school. If that's you, you need to look into **Veterans Upward Bound (VUB)** at [Moreno Valley College](https://www.moval.living/business/moreno-valley-college-dpBieY).

MVC is one of the few colleges in the nation with a federal grant specifically for this. It's 100% free, and they help you refresh your academic skills — math, English, science — before you even start your degree. They also have a dedicated **Veterans Resource Center (VRC)** that is consistently ranked as one of the "Best for Vets" in the country.

One of the best parts? They actually give you credit for what you did in the service. They've awarded credit for hundreds of Joint Services Transcripts, which means you don't have to waste time repeating things you already mastered in the military. Plus, the VRC gives you a place to hang out with other student-veterans, which makes the whole college experience feel a lot less like being an outsider.

## Navigating the "Alphabet Soup" of Benefits

We all know the VA can be a mountain of paperwork. My advice: **don't try to climb it alone.**

The **Riverside County Veterans' Services Office** is the one number you should have saved in your phone. They have accredited officers — many of them veterans themselves — who act as your advocate for free. They'll help you file disability claims, request replacement DD-214s, and even help your kids get the **CalVet College Fee Waiver** (for your dependents, not you).

Speaking of CalVet, don't forget that California has its own set of benefits separate from the federal VA. Whether it's education, home loans, or even just getting that "VETERAN" designation on your driver's license, the county office can walk you through it.

And for healthcare, we're lucky to be so close to the **Jerry L. Pettis Memorial Veterans' Hospital** in Loma Linda. It's a full-service VA hospital with a 24/7 emergency room. If you don't want to drive all the way there, they have outpatient clinics popping up all over the Inland Empire that handle primary care and mental health through telehealth.

## Finding a Home and Helping Others

If you're looking to buy a home, remember that the **VA home loan** is hands-down the best benefit we earned. In a place like MoVal, being able to buy with **no down payment** and no monthly mortgage insurance is a game-changer.

But I also know that not every vet is in a position to buy a home right now. Some of our brothers and sisters are struggling. If you or someone you know is facing housing issues or homelessness, **U.S.VETS Inland Empire** is based right out of March ARB. They've been here for over twenty years providing emergency and permanent housing, mental health support, and career help. They are a lifeline for veterans who feel like they've reached a dead end.

## More Than Just a Bar: The Veteran Community

This is the part that's most personal to me. Government programs are great, but they can't replace the camaraderie we had in the service.

I'm on the Executive Board for [American Legion Post 574](https://www.moval.living/business/american-legion-post-574-yT7WQI) here in Moreno Valley. I'll be the first to tell you: some people think the American Legion is just a smoky bar for old-timers to complain. But that's a myth. The bar is just how we keep the lights on so we can do the real work. We're focused on community awareness and supporting our own.

We also have [VFW Post 8547](https://www.moval.living/business/veterans-of-foreign-wars-alessandro-post-8547-ArCJTZ) on Alessandro Boulevard. These organizations are about advocacy, service projects, and just having a place where people "get it."

If you're looking for a way to give back that's actually fun, check out the **March Field Air Museum**. My wife is a volunteer docent there, and while she isn't a vet, she absolutely loves the environment. There are tons of veterans who volunteer there, and they are always looking for more hands to help preserve the history of the base that defined this town. It's a great way to stay productive and stay connected to our military roots.

I also spend time on the [Moreno Valley Chamber of Commerce](https://www.moval.living/business/moreno-valley-chamber-of-commerce-ao1b2h) Military Affairs committee. We meet once a month to figure out how to bridge the gap between local businesses and the veteran community. It's proof that this city actually cares about us — not just on Veterans Day, but every single month.

## A Legacy of Service

If you need a quiet place to reflect, head over to the **Moreno Valley Veterans Memorial** at the Civic Center on Frederick Street. It's open 24/7, and it's a powerful place with granite panels honoring those from our community who didn't make it home. And, of course, we have **Riverside National Cemetery** right next door, which is one of the most beautiful and active national cemeteries in the country.

At the end of the day, leaving the military isn't about leaving your community behind — it's about finding your next one. Moreno Valley has built an ecosystem where we can thrive, whether you're looking to start a second career, get a degree, or just find a group of people who understand the life.

Military service may have ended, but our sense of service to each other doesn't have to. If you're doing well, reach out and mentor a younger vet. If you're struggling, reach out to one of these organizations. We've got each other's backs here in MoVal.

See you at the Post.
