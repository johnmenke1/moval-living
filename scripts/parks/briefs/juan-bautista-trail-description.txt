The Juan Bautista de Anza Multi-Use Trail is an approximately 8-mile paved, ADA-compliant path that runs diagonally across Moreno Valley from Towngate Boulevard in the north to the Lake Perris State Recreation Area in the south. Built in phases and fully completed in 2023 with funding from the California Transportation Commission's Active Transportation Program, the trail sits on an otherwise unusable 100-foot-wide California Department of Water Resources (DWR) water pipeline easement — land where no structures are permitted, but where the City worked cooperatively with DWR, California State Parks, and the Riverside County Flood Control District to install non-structural recreational improvements.

The trail is part of the Juan Bautista de Anza National Historic Trail, a 1,200-mile route administered by the National Park Service that follows the 1775-76 land expedition of Spanish military leader Juan Bautista de Anza from Sonora, Mexico to San Francisco. The Moreno Valley segment was officially certified and dedicated on July 24, 2024 at John F. Kennedy Veterans Memorial Park, receiving a commemorative plaque and certificate from the NPS.

The paved, mostly flat route links multiple neighborhoods, nine parks, and at least four schools, with intersection safety improvements and ramps designed to encourage students to walk and bike to class. Designers used recycled materials in the asphalt and concrete, drought-tolerant landscaping, and energy-saving LED lighting. Features include ADA-compliant ramps, LED safety lighting, low-impact native landscaping, and interpretive/historical markers tied to the Anza expedition. The trail is open to pedestrians, bicyclists, and equestrians, with leashed dogs permitted; bicyclists must yield to pedestrians and equestrians.
